# Personas

These personas are illustrative composites, not real individuals, used to
drive design and content decisions. Each maps to at least one terminal
outcome in `04-decision-tree-spec.md`. Forms/paths cited follow the
post-2022 consolidated form set (Form 6, 6A, 7, 8) — verify current
requirements against voters.eci.gov.in before publishing any user-facing
copy derived from these personas.

## 1. Aarav — First-time voter, just turned 18

- Age: 18
- Situation: Turned 18 two months ago, lives with parents in the same
  house he's grown up in, has never registered to vote.
- Goal: Register as a new elector before the next election in his state.
- Barriers: Doesn't know if he can register immediately or must wait for a
  specific date; unsure what documents count as address/age proof.
- Correct form/path: Form 6 (new elector registration), after confirming he
  has reached the next applicable qualifying date (1 January, 1 April,
  1 July, or 1 October following his 18th birthday).
- Quote: "I turned 18 in May. Can I register now, or do I have to wait a
  whole year?"

## 2. Priya — Engineering student in a hostel

- Age: 19
- Situation: From a small town in Odisha, studying at a college in
  Bengaluru, lives in a college hostel for 10 months of the year.
- Goal: Decide whether to register at her hostel address (Bengaluru) or
  her family home address (Odisha), and register correctly either way.
- Barriers: Doesn't know registering away from home is even allowed;
  doesn't know she needs a bonafide certificate from her Head of
  Institution if she registers at the hostel address.
- Correct form/path: Form 6, with the "ordinary residence" choice — either
  address is valid; if choosing the hostel address, she needs a bonafide
  certificate (course recognized by a Central/State body or University,
  minimum 1 year duration) plus proof of ordinary residence at that
  address.
- Quote: "I live here nine months a year — can I vote here instead of
  going home every election?"

## 3. Mohammed — Migrant construction worker

- Age: 34
- Situation: Moved from Bihar to Maharashtra for construction work three
  years ago; still registered at his native address; rarely able to
  travel back during elections.
- Goal: Understand whether he should shift his registration to
  Maharashtra or keep it in Bihar, and what's involved either way.
- Barriers: Limited literacy in the state language of his new home; no
  fixed formal rental agreement (informal housing arrangement); worried
  about losing his existing registration if he tries to change it.
- Correct form/path: Form 8 (shifting of residence to a different AC) if
  he wants to register in Maharashtra and can show proof of ordinary
  residence there; otherwise he may remain registered at his native
  address and use postal/other applicable provisions only if he qualifies
  as a special category (verify — migrant workers are not automatically a
  postal-ballot category; confirm against current ECI rules before making
  this claim in-product).
- Quote: "I've been here three years but my vote is still back home. What
  changes if I move it?"

## 4. Kavita — Married, moved after wedding

- Age: 27
- Situation: Got married eight months ago and moved from her parents'
  house in Lucknow to her husband's family home in Kanpur.
- Goal: Update her voter registration to her new address and, ideally,
  her name if she has changed it.
- Barriers: Confusion about whether this is a "correction" or a "new
  registration"; unsure which documents prove her new address.
- Correct form/path: Form 8 — shifting of residence (cross-AC, Lucknow to
  Kanpur) and, in the same form, correction of entries if her name has
  changed.
- Quote: "Do I need a whole new voter ID because I moved after marriage,
  or can I just update the old one?"

## 5. Rahul — Transferred government employee

- Age: 41
- Situation: Central government employee, transferred from Delhi to
  Chennai on a 3-year posting; expects to be transferred again later.
- Goal: Decide whether it's worth changing his registration for a
  temporary posting.
- Barriers: Doesn't know if there's a simpler path for employees who
  relocate frequently; worried about re-doing this every few years.
- Correct form/path: Form 8 (shifting of residence) if he chooses to
  register at the posting address; general employees (non-service-voter
  categories) do not get an automatic simplified path — verify against
  current ECI service-voter category rules before asserting otherwise.
- Quote: "I'll probably be transferred again in three years. Is it even
  worth changing my registration?"

## 6. Ananya — NRI voter, first registration

- Age: 24
- Situation: Indian citizen, moved to Canada for a master's degree and a
  subsequent job; has never registered to vote in India; retains Indian
  citizenship (has not acquired foreign citizenship).
- Goal: Register as an overseas elector using her Indian passport details.
- Barriers: Doesn't know overseas registration is a separate form; unsure
  if she needs to be physically present in India to register or to vote.
- Correct form/path: Form 6A (registration of overseas/NRI electors),
  registered at the address in India shown in her passport; voting
  currently requires in-person presence at the polling station in India
  (verify current status of any overseas/postal voting facility for NRIs
  against ECI before asserting either way — this has been a changing
  policy area).
- Quote: "I still have my Indian passport — how do I register to vote from
  abroad?"

## 7. Colonel Verma (retd.) — Service voter category, now settled

- Age: 58
- Situation: Retired from the Army five years ago after being registered
  for years as a service voter; now permanently settled in Pune.
- Goal: Move off the service-voter category and register as an ordinary
  elector at his permanent Pune address.
- Barriers: Unsure whether his old service-voter entry needs to be
  formally removed, or whether it lapses automatically.
- Correct form/path: Likely Form 6 (new ordinary registration) at his Pune
  address, and Form 7 (objection to inclusion) may be relevant to have his
  prior service-voter entry removed if it isn't automatically superseded —
  verify the exact transition process against ECI's service-voter rules
  before publishing definitive guidance; flag as "confirm with your ERO"
  in-product if uncertain.
- Quote: "I'm not in the forces anymore. Do I need to formally cancel my
  old service-vote entry?"

## 8. Deepa — PwD elector requesting home voting

- Age: 52
- Situation: Wheelchair user due to a spinal condition; registered voter
  in Chennai; physically unable to visit her polling station easily.
- Goal: Get her PwD status marked on the electoral roll and request home
  voting for the next election.
- Barriers: Doesn't know PwD marking and home voting are two separate
  steps with different timing; unaware of the 5-day submission window for
  Form 12D after election notification.
- Correct form/path: Form 8 to mark PwD status on the roll (can be done
  any time), then Form 12D submitted to the Returning Officer within 5
  days of election notification to request postal ballot / home voting.
- Quote: "I want to vote from home this time — what do I need to submit,
  and by when?"

## 9. Kamla Devi — Senior citizen, lost EPIC

- Age: 79
- Situation: Long-registered voter; her physical EPIC card was lost during
  a house move; not very comfortable with smartphones.
- Goal: Get a replacement voter ID, or find another way to prove she's
  registered.
- Barriers: Limited digital literacy; may not have a smartphone/email for
  OTP verification; needs a family member or BLO's help.
- Correct form/path: Form 8 (replacement/duplicate EPIC), or, if she or a
  family member can complete OTP verification, download the e-EPIC
  (digital voter ID, legally equivalent to the physical card) from
  voters.eci.gov.in. She is also within the age band (85+) potentially
  eligible for home voting via Form 12D in future elections, once she
  reaches that threshold — separately from the EPIC replacement issue at
  hand now.
- Quote: "My card is gone. Can someone bring me a new one, or does it all
  have to be done online?"

## 10. Suresh — Name spelled wrong on EPIC

- Age: 45
- Situation: Registered voter for 20 years; his name has always been
  misspelled on his EPIC ("Suersh" instead of "Suresh"); has never
  corrected it because it seemed like a hassle.
- Goal: Correct the spelling of his name on the electoral roll.
- Barriers: Assumes correcting his name will require re-registering from
  scratch and losing his voting history/continuity.
- Correct form/path: Form 8 (correction of entries — name), no
  re-registration needed.
- Quote: "It's been wrong for twenty years. Is fixing it actually simple,
  or do I have to start over?"

## 11. Farida — Found a duplicate entry for her mother

- Age: 30
- Situation: While checking the electoral roll for her family, found her
  late mother still listed as a registered elector, and also found what
  appears to be a duplicate entry for herself in two different
  constituencies.
- Goal: Have her mother's name removed and resolve her own duplicate
  registration.
- Barriers: Doesn't know objection/deletion is a different form from
  correction; nervous about "getting in trouble" for the duplicate, even
  though it wasn't intentional.
- Correct form/path: Form 7 (objection to inclusion of a name / claim for
  deletion) for both the deceased-relative case and the duplicate-entry
  case.
- Quote: "My mother passed away two years ago — she's still on the list.
  And somehow I seem to be listed twice."

## 12. Noor — Transgender citizen, updating gender and address

- Age: 26
- Situation: Registered several years ago under a different gender marker
  and an old address; has since moved to a new city.
- Goal: Update gender marker and address on the electoral roll accurately
  and with dignity.
- Barriers: Concerned about being asked for invasive or unavailable
  documentation; unsure whether gender correction and address change can
  be done in one step.
- Correct form/path: Form 8 covers both correction of entries (including
  gender) and shifting of residence; these can generally be requested
  together on the same form (verify current combined-form workflow and
  documentation requirements against voters.eci.gov.in / ECI circulars on
  transgender elector registration before publishing specific document
  lists).
- Quote: "I just want my details to be correct and be done with it, without
  having to explain myself over and over."

## 13. Birsa — Resident of a remote tribal block

- Age: 36
- Situation: Lives in a remote tribal block in Jharkhand with limited
  internet connectivity and the nearest BLO/ERO office a significant
  distance away; wants to check if his family's registration is current
  after a local boundary/constituency change.
- Goal: Confirm his correct constituency and polling station, and resolve
  any roll discrepancies, without relying on continuous internet access.
- Barriers: Low/intermittent connectivity rules out assuming the user can
  complete an online flow in one sitting; may rely on the Voter Helpline
  app's offline-tolerant features, a visiting BLO, or the 1950 helpline
  instead of self-service web.
- Correct form/path: Roll search / polling station locator first; Form 8
  for any correction needed once the discrepancy is identified. In-product
  guidance should proactively surface the BLO-contact and 1950 helpline
  paths as equally valid alternatives to the web flow, not as a fallback
  bolted on afterward.
- Quote: "The nearest office is far and my phone barely gets signal. Can I
  sort this out over a phone call instead?"

## 14. Nasreen — No Aadhaar, no passport, no fixed address proof

- Age: 40
- Situation: Works informally, lives in an urban settlement without a
  fixed formal address document (no ownership papers, no long-term rental
  agreement); has an Aadhaar but no other standard ID.
- Goal: Register to vote despite lacking the "standard" bundle of
  documents typically listed for address/identity proof.
- Barriers: Believes (incorrectly, or correctly — needs verification) that
  a complete standard document set is mandatory; unaware of alternative
  accepted proofs or the BLO-verification pathway that may substitute for
  paper documents in some cases.
- Correct form/path: Form 6, with guidance that the exact list of
  acceptable ID/address proofs (and any alternative verification route via
  local BLO enquiry) must be confirmed against the current Form 6
  instructions on voters.eci.gov.in — this persona is explicitly flagged
  as "verify against official source before asserting a specific document
  list" rather than a source of invented specifics.
- Quote: "I don't have most of the documents people usually ask for. Does
  that mean I just can't register?"
